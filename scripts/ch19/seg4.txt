So the Jivatma must think over his likes and dislikes, and train himself to direct his emotions rightly. Emotions guided by reason, in accordance with the will of Ishvara, develop into virtues, and the culture of the emotions is the ethical development of man. The Bhagavad Gita warns that affection and aversion abide in the senses, and are obstructors of the path. But the disciplined self, moving among sense objects with senses free from attraction and repulsion, mastered by the self, goes to peace. Desire and anger, the Gita says, arise out of Rajas, and following desire alone brings neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the highest goal.
