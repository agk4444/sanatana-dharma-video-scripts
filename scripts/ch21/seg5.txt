The fowler who knows Brahman tells the Brahmana Kaushika that his parents are the idols he worships, like the three sacred fires, and that no virtue is higher than honouring them. Bhishma gives up throne and marriage so his father may wed Satyavati, and wins the boon of dying only at his own will. But Duryodhana's disobedience to his parents precipitated the war that destroyed his house. The teacher stands beside father and mother as a third great object of reverence, and the aged close the list, for their wisdom is the fruit of long experience, and saluting them brings length of life, knowledge, fame, and strength.
