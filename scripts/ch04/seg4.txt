But are we then helplessly bound? The chapter says: true, but only to a certain extent. We are always changing, as we gain new experiences, and the Jiva gains more knowledge and changes his desires. A man who has acted cruelly sees that his cruelty made others hate and fear him, and made him lonely and unhappy. He resolves to change, and goes to the root of the trouble — the desire itself. Instead of letting desires carry him away like runaway horses, he uses thought as a bridle, and keeps his desires in check. Liberation is not won by fleeing to the forest. King Janaka kept his kingdom and wealth, yet said that if all Mithila burned, nothing would be lost to him. The merchant Tuladhara reached the same freedom by harming none, asking no loan, and quarrelling with none.
