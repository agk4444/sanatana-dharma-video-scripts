The book begins with a seed. Plant a seed, and it sends up a stem; leaves grow, flowers come, then fruits, and in the fruits are seeds again. The same seed always gives the same plant: rice gives rice, wheat gives wheat, thistle gives thistle. Sow thistles, and you must not expect sweet grapes; plant prickly cactus, and you must not expect juicy apples. This is karma: knowing it, a man sows the seed of the thing he wants to reap. And an action is not so simple as it looks. Behind every action stands a thought, and behind every thought a desire. Desire, thought, action — three threads twisted into the cord of karma.
