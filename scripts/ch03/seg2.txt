The Jiva, the book says, is a portion of Brahman. Shri Krishna calls him "a portion of myself, a Jiva," and the Shruti teaches, "Thou art That." The book gives the image of a seed and a tree. A tree drops a seed carrying its own nature, and the seed grows until it becomes a tree like its parent. So the Jiva is dropped into matter by Ishvara, and grows, putting out his hidden powers, until he becomes Ishvara. His pilgrimage begins in the mineral kingdom, unconscious of the outer world, until earthquakes, volcanoes and the furious surf awaken him. Then he passes into plants, feeling the warm sun and the rain, and then into animals, where hunting and outwitting bring out his senses, until no animal form is good enough, and he needs the human.
