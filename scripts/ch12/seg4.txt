The household stage is entered at marriage, and of all the Ashramas it is the most important, for it supports all the others. The welfare of family and nation depends on the householder, and a good husband, a good father, a good master, and a good citizen is the noblest of men. The home is the school of unselfishness, compassion, tenderness, and charity. When some well-born youths abandoned their homes too early for the forest, Indra pitied them and, in the form of a golden bird, taught them: "Follow the household life! It is the field for the cultivation of virtues. It is sacred." To eat the remnant of food left after helping the needy is to eat the true remnant of sacrifice. Plucking unripe fruit deprives the eater of its sweetness.
