The fifth is the sacrifice to the Bhutas, to creatures: a little food placed on the ground before the meal, and the remains of the meal left in a suitable place for vagrant men and animals. Its inner meaning is the duty of caring for those beneath us in the human and the lower kingdoms, and the practice of kindness and consideration towards them. So the five daily sacrifices teach a man his relations with all around him, with his superiors, his equals, and his inferiors. They turn the wheel of life in accord with the will of Ishvara, and they teach each of us that we are not an isolated unit, but a part of a great whole, a cell in a vast body.
