Sometimes, the chapter says, the inferior may save his superior from falling into sin through wrath and pride. Chirakarin, whose name means acting slowly, thought long when his angry father ordered him to slay his mother, and by his patience did his father's real will, not his hasty order, saving his father from a grievous sin. So the teaching closes as it began, with gentleness: created beings must be instructed without giving them pain, and sweet and gentle speech must be used by a superior who desires to fulfil the sacred law.
