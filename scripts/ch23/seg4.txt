But there is a danger in showing tenderness and protection to those weaker than ourselves: the vice of pride. Pride arises from ahamkara, the sense of separateness of "I" and "you," which thinks more of the fact that "I am helping this weaker one" than of sharing what is really a common store. By dwelling on one's own usefulness, pride is awakened, and quickly ruins the good work performed. Even the very highest fall beneath its sway. The sage Narayana, proud of his power, was drawn into a promise of marriage; Vishvamitra's greed for Vashishtha's cow led to a war that changed the history of the land; Indra, who would not rise to greet his teacher, lost his throne; Nahusha, set to rule heaven in Indra's place, grew proud and fell to earth as a serpent; and Bali lost the goddess of prosperity when pride entered his heart.
