This duty of protecting the weak is incarnated in the righteous king, and it is the fulfilment of this duty which awakens the loyalty of his subjects. "To protect his subjects is the cream of kingly duties," says Bhishma, and a king should behave toward his subjects as a mother toward the child of her womb. Yudhishthira would not enter heaven without the faithful dog that had followed him through the desert, saying it would be a sin to abandon one so devoted. King Shibi gave flesh from his own body, and at last his whole body, to save a dove that had sought his refuge from a hawk. And Rantideva, after fasting for forty-eight days, gave away his first meal, share by share, and finally his last drink of water to a suffering outcaste, praying only that he might suffer for all beings, that they might live without sorrow.
