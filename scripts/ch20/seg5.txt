Selfish desire is a source of evil and sorrow: peace arises from the absence of desires, not from their gratification. Manki, greedy of wealth, was ever disappointed, until the loss of his last pair of calves opened his heart, and he sang: He that desires happiness must renounce desire. Thus Manki lost a little, and gained all. King Yayati borrowed his son Puru's youth for a thousand years to exhaust the pleasures of the senses, but his craving only grew, until he saw that desire ceases not with satisfaction but with frustration, and retired chanting: Desire may not be quenched by enjoyment of its objects; it only increases manifold therewith, as fire with libations of butter. And harmlessness is the highest Dharma, Bhishma taught: we should injure nothing. Bhima's boyish rough play planted a smouldering fire of dislike that grew into the blazing hate that consumed Kaurava and Pandava alike. The triple control of mind, speech and body results in righteousness. All the virtues have their root in pure Love, and have Bliss for their fruit; all the vices have their root in personal Hate, and their fruit is Misery.
