Purity reaches deeper than the visible body. The Pranamayakosha, the invisible double of the body, depends on magnetic currents: onions and garlic harm it, alcohol harms it, and so do the vital bodies of others — hence the danger of bad company. And the Manomayakosha depends on the purity of its owner's thoughts and desires, the most fertile source of impurity of all. A man may observe the rules of Shaucha to the last point of strictness, but if he be proud, passionate, harsh, vain, suspicious, he is pouring impurity in faster than any rules can wash it out. In the eyes of the Rishis and the Devas, such a one is ever ashuchi — impure. The chapter closes with the words of Manu: the body is purified by water, the mind by truth, the soul by knowledge and austerity, the reason by wisdom. Next: Chapter 10 — The Five Daily Sacrifices.
