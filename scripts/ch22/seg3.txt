Then Savitri, who won her husband back from Yama, the King of Death. Told that Satyavan would die within a year, she marries him all the same, saying, but once can a person give herself away, and I have said it once. She fasts three days, follows Yama as he carries her husband's soul away, and wins boon after boon with wise and gentle words: her father-in-law's sight, his kingdom, a hundred sons for her father and a hundred for herself, until Yama grants her husband's life itself. Even Yama, King of Death, the book says, is weaker than a pure wife's faithful love. And Damayanti, too, clings to Nala through exile and hunger, for in every sorrow there is no such medicine as a loving and faithful wife.
