Outside the family, hospitality stands in the first rank. A poor Brahmana, living on grains gathered in famine, gives his scanty barley, share by share, to a guest, until the guest is revealed as Dharma himself, and a mongoose rolling in the grains left uneaten turns half to gold. And forgiveness marks a truly noble nature: Rama forgets a hundred injuries and remembers one benefit, and Vidura, driven away with insult by his brother, returns at once when called, saying simply, O King, I have forgiven. So the book teaches gentle speech to equals, for there are no riches like compassion, friendliness, charity, and sweet speech. Next: Chapter 23 — Virtues and Vices in relation to Inferiors.
