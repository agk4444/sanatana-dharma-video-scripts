Worship may take many forms: praise of His perfection, prayer rooted in a sense of imperfection, appeal to His love, recognition of His power, meditation on His nature, intense longing for His unveiling, according to the temperament and stage of the worshipper. But in peasant or philosopher, the root-longing is the same. The All, the Unconditioned, is never an object of worship; attributes are needed, on which the mind can be fixed. So it is Saguna Brahman, Ishvara, whom all prayers reach. He may be adored as Shiva or Vishnu, Durga or Lakshmi, Ganesha or Sarasvati, or as an Avatara — Rama, Krishna, or Buddha — but under whatever name and form, it is Ishvara who is worshipped.
