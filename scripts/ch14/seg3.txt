The object of morality, the chapter says, is to secure the welfare of all beings — by showing men, through the science of Conduct, how to live in harmony with each other, and with their surroundings. Ishvara is Love, and He wills the happiness of His universe, and is gradually bringing about universal happiness. That does not mean a right thing is always pleasant at the time, or a wrong thing always unpleasant. It means the conduct that leads to lasting happiness — to the bliss of union with Ishvara, to Moksha in the end — is good. As the wheels of the cart follow the ox, so misery follows sin, and so also happiness follows righteousness.
