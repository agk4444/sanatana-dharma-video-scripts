Shraddha is the name of the ceremonies performed to help the Jiva, the soul, who has put off his visible body in death, by those who remain in this world. The soul that has left its body is called a Preta, and the first part of the rite is called the Preta-kriya. The visible body, the Annamayakosha, is carried to the burning-ground, so that it may dissolve into its constituents as quickly as possible. The ashes are gathered on the third day and thrown into running water, preferably into a sacred stream. Burning, the book says, is the best way of destroying a dead body. When the body is destroyed, the Pranamayakosha, the life-sheath, rapidly disperses, quickened by the mantras used at the cremation.
